How AAC talking tiles work — tap a tile, it speaks for you

When speech is hard, a board of tiles can carry the words. Tap one and it says a phrase out loud; chain a few and it speaks a whole sentence. Here is exactly how that works — and why a well-built one never sends a word anywhere.

01

The problem: the words are there, the voice isn't

After a stroke, with aphasia, for an autistic child still finding speech, or after a laryngectomy — the thoughts are whole, but saying them aloud is the part that breaks. Pointing, writing, or waiting for someone to guess is slow and exhausting. A communication board turns knowing what you want to say into one tap.

I want to say “water”
…but it comes out — silence —

A board bridges that gap. It doesn't invent the words; it just gives them a voice.

02

How it works: a tile is a phrase, plus a picture, plus a voice

Every tile holds three things — an emoji you can recognise at a glance, a short label to read, and the full phrase it speaks. Tap it and the device says the whole phrase aloud. The coral arcs are the visible “it's talking now” signal, so the feedback isn't sound-only.

These are live. Tap a tile — if your device has a voice installed, it will speak. Otherwise, watch the arcs and caption.

03

Chaining tiles: the sentence strip

One tile is a phrase. For anything longer, tiles drop onto a sentence strip: tap, tap, tap to build a line, then press Speak and the whole sentence is read at once. It concatenates short words — no grammar engine pretending to conjugate — which keeps it honest and predictable.

I want water please
Speak

The strip says exactly what's on it — “I want water please” — in the order you tapped. Backspace fixes a slip; clear starts over.

04

The colour key: a thin stripe that speeds recognition

Each tile carries a slim edge-stripe following the modified Fitzgerald Key, a long-standing AAC convention (Thistle & Wilkinson, 2009): people, actions, describing words, things, and social words each get a consistent colour. Over time the colour itself becomes a shortcut to the right part of the board. The stripe is deliberately muted — never the only signal, always paired with the word and picture.

  • PeopleI, you, mum, nurse
  • Actionswant, go, help, stop
  • Describinghot, tired, more, bad
  • Thingswater, food, phone
  • Socialyes, please, thank you
  • Questionswho, what, where

Colour is a helper, not a code you must memorise: state is never colour-only — the label is always right there.

05

Private by construction: the browser blocks the exit

A communication board holds some of the most personal things a person says. Most “private” apps ask you to trust a promise. talktiles makes the browser enforce it: the page ships a Content-Security-Policy with connect-src 'none'. That single line means the page cannot open a network connection — no upload, no analytics, no “phone home” — even if it tried.
